<objective>
Give Epiphan BDRs a ready-to-send email/SMS cadence that earns replies without
burning the lead. This is the WRITTEN sibling of epiphan-call-playbook (phone).
Every touch leads with the buyer's problem, asks exactly one question, and holds
back the product name until the prospect has engaged.

Core rule (from epiphan-call-playbook): never just send info. Earn one
qualifying answer first, then send the info email using the matching template.
</objective>

<quick_start>
1. Identify the vertical (Higher Ed, Courts/Legal, Government, Healthcare,
   Corporate AV, Houses of Worship, K-12) and the personalization hook
   (org name + a known trigger: budget cycle, new building, accreditation,
   Extron SMP EOL, a failed recording).
2. Pull the Touch 1 template for that vertical. Fill the [brackets].
3. Enroll into the 5-touch cadence (Nooks handles enrollment; this skill is
   the CONTENT for each step). Send-from is always tkipper@epiphan.com.
4. Create Gmail drafts for review before send (Tim's workflow: draft →
   review/edit → send).
5. When a prospect replies with a qualifying answer, STOP the cadence and send
   the tailored info email — or hand to epiphan-call-playbook for the call.
</quick_start>

<success_criteria>
- Touch 1 subject line names the buyer's problem, NOT a product.
- Exactly one question per email. Zero emails with two asks.
- SMS is max 2 sentences and always opens with the first name.
- Every email signs: Tim Kipper | Sr. BDR, Epiphan Video | tkipper@epiphan.com
- Closes are soft and permission-based — drawn from the Soft Close Phrases bank. No hard "book a call" CTAs on cold touches.
- Personalization hook present in Touch 1 and Touch 2 (org name + trigger).
- Cadence stops the moment a reply lands. No touch fires after engagement.
</success_criteria>

## 5-Touch Cadence Overview

| Touch | Day | Channel | Job | Question |
|-------|-----|---------|-----|----------|
| 1 | 1 | Email | Challenger insight + diagnostic | One diagnostic Q, no product |
| 2 | 3 | Email | Add vertical stat/case, re-ask | Re-ask, reframed |
| 3 | 5 | SMS or short email | Pattern interrupt, ask permission | "Worth a look, or wrong time?" |
| 4 | 8 | Email | Value-add (spec compare, doc) | Soft CTA, low-commitment |
| 5 | 12 | Email | Break-up | "Say the word and I'll close this out" |

Methodology: Challenger (Teach → Tailor → Take Control) applied to writing.
Touch 1 TEACHES a reframe. Touch 2 TAILORS with a vertical proof point. Touches
3-5 TAKE CONTROL by making the next step obvious and easy.

Discovery through the whole cadence, borrow Tim's proven line where natural:
"Where do you tend to lose the most time — capture, switching sources, or
getting recordings out the door?"

## Touch 1: Cold Email (Day 1)

Teach a reframe. One diagnostic question. No product name. No product name in
the subject line either.

### Higher Ed template

> **Subject:** Lecture capture that survives finals week
>
> Hi [First Name],
>
> Most [University Name] teams I talk to don't lose lecture capture to budget —
> they lose it to reliability. A software encoder on a classroom PC works great
> until the one week enrollment doubles and it drops a recording nobody can get
> back.
>
> The programs that stopped firefighting moved capture off the PC entirely, so
> an unattended room records the same way every time.
>
> Where do you tend to lose the most time right now — capture, switching
> sources, or getting recordings out the door?
>
> Tim Kipper | Sr. BDR, Epiphan Video | tkipper@epiphan.com

### Courts template

> **Subject:** When the record fails, who owns it?
>
> Hi [First Name],
>
> For a court, a dropped recording isn't an inconvenience — it's a gap in the
> official record. Most [Court/County Name] setups I see still run on software
> that was never built to record unattended for eight hours straight.
>
> The clerks who sleep at night moved to dedicated hardware that records the
> same way every session, with no PC in the loop to crash mid-hearing.
>
> How are you handling it today when a recording drops in the middle of a
> proceeding?
>
> Tim Kipper | Sr. BDR, Epiphan Video | tkipper@epiphan.com

### Government template

> **Subject:** [Meeting] recordings without the AV headcount
>
> Hi [First Name],
>
> [Agency/City Name] meetings have to be captured and posted whether or not an
> AV person is in the room that day. Most teams I talk to are one sick day away
> from a missed council recording.
>
> The move that fixes it: capture that runs unattended and hands you the file
> automatically, so coverage doesn't depend on who's on shift.
>
> What's your fallback today when nobody's available to run the recording?
>
> Tim Kipper | Sr. BDR, Epiphan Video | tkipper@epiphan.com

### Healthcare template

> **Subject:** Capturing the OR without adding IT load
>
> Hi [First Name],
>
> In medical education the hard part isn't recording the procedure — it's doing
> it reliably without pulling clinical IT into every session. Most [Hospital/
> System Name] teams I speak with are stitching together software that needs a
> babysitter.
>
> The programs that scaled moved to dedicated capture that runs itself, so
> med-ed doesn't compete with clinical systems for attention.
>
> Where's the friction today — capturing the procedure, switching between
> sources, or getting the recording to the learners?
>
> Tim Kipper | Sr. BDR, Epiphan Video | tkipper@epiphan.com

### Generic / Corporate AV pattern

Fill-in-the-blank:

> **Subject:** [Buyer problem, 4-7 words, no product]
>
> Hi [First Name],
>
> Most [Company Name] teams I talk to don't lose [outcome] to [obvious
> suspect] — they lose it to [real root cause]. [One concrete failure scenario
> the buyer will recognize.]
>
> The teams that stopped [firefighting behavior] moved to [reframed approach,
> still no product name].
>
> [One diagnostic question — where do they lose the most time?]
>
> Tim Kipper | Sr. BDR, Epiphan Video | tkipper@epiphan.com

## Touch 2: Follow-Up (Day 3)

Add a vertical-specific proof point or stat, then re-ask the Touch 1 question
reframed. Reference the original thread lightly; don't apologize for following
up.

Pattern:

> **Subject:** Re: [Touch 1 subject]  *(or a fresh 4-7 word problem line)*
>
> Hi [First Name],
>
> Quick follow-up with something concrete: [vertical stat or one-line case
> reference — e.g. "a [peer org type] moved 40 rooms off classroom PCs and
> hasn't lost a recording in two semesters"].
>
> Curious how that maps to [Org Name] — [re-ask the diagnostic, reframed:
> "is reliability actually the pain, or is it more about getting recordings
> distributed after the fact?"].
>
> Tim Kipper | Sr. BDR, Epiphan Video | tkipper@epiphan.com

Proof-point bank (swap by vertical):
- **Higher Ed:** peer institution moved N rooms off classroom PCs, zero lost
  recordings across two semesters.
- **Courts:** county replaced software recording with dedicated hardware after
  a dropped-record incident; clean record every session since.
- **Government:** city automated council capture; coverage no longer depends on
  who's on shift.
- **Healthcare:** med-ed program scaled OR capture without adding clinical IT
  tickets.
- **Extron SMP angle (any vertical):** "Extron discontinued the SMP line — a
  lot of teams are quietly asking what happens when support ends. Worth getting
  ahead of it."

## Touch 3: SMS / Short Email (Day 5)

Pattern interrupt. Acknowledge the silence, ask permission to continue. If SMS:
max 2 sentences, open with the first name.

**SMS option:**

> [First Name] — Tim from Epiphan here, I sent a couple notes on making [capture/
> recording] bulletproof. Worth a quick look, or is this just the wrong time?

**Short-email option:**

> **Subject:** Wrong time, or worth a look?
>
> Hi [First Name],
>
> No worries if this isn't a priority right now — just want to make sure I'm not
> chasing a dead end.
>
> Is [reliable capture / the recording gap] something on your radar this
> quarter, or should I circle back later?
>
> Tim Kipper | Sr. BDR, Epiphan Video | tkipper@epiphan.com

## Touch 4: Value-Add (Day 8)

Give something useful with no strings — a spec comparison, a relevant doc, a
one-line differentiator. Soft, low-commitment CTA.

> **Subject:** Hardware vs software capture — the short version
>
> Hi [First Name],
>
> Regardless of where [Org Name] lands, this is the comparison that usually
> settles the debate:
>
> - **Software (vMix/OBS on a PC):** cheap, flexible, but it's sharing a
>   machine and an OS that can crash mid-record. Fine when someone's watching
>   it; risky unattended.
> - **Dedicated hardware:** one job, records the same way every time, runs
>   unattended for hours. Higher up-front cost, far lower "lost recording" cost.
>
> If it's useful I can send a one-page spec breakdown for your exact room setup —
> want me to?
>
> Tim Kipper | Sr. BDR, Epiphan Video | tkipper@epiphan.com

Value-add bank: spec comparison sheet, Extron SMP EOL migration note, a 90-second
reliability explainer, a peer-vertical case one-pager, an Epiphan Connect remote-
management overview (only if fleet/multi-room came up).

## Touch 5: Break-Up (Day 12)

Last reach out. Make it easy to say "not now" — the negative-reply close often
gets the response the other four didn't.

> **Subject:** Closing the loop
>
> Hi [First Name],
>
> I'll stop reaching out after this — the last thing I want to do is clutter
> your inbox.
>
> If reliable capture isn't a priority right now, just say the word and I'll
> close this out. And if timing's the only issue, tell me when to check back
> and I'll make a note.
>
> Either way, thanks for the read.
>
> Tim Kipper | Sr. BDR, Epiphan Video | tkipper@epiphan.com

## Subject Line Patterns

Rules: 4-7 words. No ALL CAPS. No "Just checking in." No product name in Touch 1.
Lead with the buyer's problem or a question they'd ask themselves.

Good:
- "Lecture capture that survives finals week"
- "When the record fails, who owns it?"
- "Recordings without the AV headcount"
- "Wrong time, or worth a look?"
- "Hardware vs software capture — the short version"
- "Closing the loop"

Avoid:
- "Just checking in" / "Following up" / "Touching base"
- "EPIPHAN PEARL MINI — 4K CAPTURE" (caps, product-led)
- "Quick question" (empty, overused)
- Anything with the product name in Touch 1

---

## Soft Close Phrases

Tentative, permission-based lines for asking to reconnect — open-ended, low-pressure, no pitch. Use one at the end of any touch instead of a hard CTA. Pick the one that fits the tone; don't stack them.

- If it's alright with you, I'd love to hear your take on this — maybe we could connect sometime this week.
- I don't want to intrude, but if you're open to it, I'd like to swap thoughts on this before too long.
- Whenever it's convenient for you, I'd be curious to know how you're thinking about this.
- If you're up for it, I'd appreciate the chance to pick your brain on this at some point.
- No pressure at all, but I'd value hearing where your head's at on this whenever you have a moment.
- If you don't mind me asking, I'd like to understand how you're approaching this — maybe we could find some time soon.
- I'd be grateful for your thoughts here, whenever works for you.
- If it's not too much trouble, I'd like to learn more about how you see this playing out.
- Would you be open to sharing your view sometime? I'd like to follow up when it's convenient.
- If you have a few minutes at some point, I'd genuinely like to hear your perspective.
- I'm curious how you'd approach this — maybe we could chat whenever your schedule allows.
- If you're comfortable with it, I'd like to get a better sense of your thinking, perhaps later this week.
- Whenever suits you, I'd welcome the opportunity to hear more about your perspective.
- If it works on your end, I'd like to circle back and learn more about how you're seeing things.
- I'd love to know your thoughts on this, if you're willing to share — happy to follow up whenever's good for you.

## Writing Rules

1. **No product name in Touch 1 subject or body.** Lead with the problem. The
   product enters only once the prospect has engaged (Touch 4 at the earliest,
   or a reply).
2. **One question per email. Never two.** If you have two, cut to the sharper one.
3. **Use soft closes, not hard CTAs.** End emails with a phrase from the Soft
   Close Phrases bank above — tentative, permission-based, no pitch. Never
   "Book a 30-minute call here" as a first touch.
4. **SMS: max 2 sentences, always start with the first name.**
5. **Subject lines: 4-7 words, no ALL CAPS, no "Just checking in."**
6. **Personalization hook required in Touch 1 and Touch 2** — org name plus a
   known trigger (budget cycle, new building, accreditation, Extron SMP EOL,
   a failed recording, a job posting for AV staff).
7. **Every email signs:** `Tim Kipper | Sr. BDR, Epiphan Video | tkipper@epiphan.com`
8. **Send-from is always tkipper@epiphan.com.** Never any other sender.
9. **Teach → Tailor → Take Control.** Touch 1 teaches, Touch 2 tailors, 3-5 take
   control.
10. **Stop on reply.** The moment a qualifying answer lands, kill the cadence and
    send the tailored info email or route to the call playbook.
11. **Never just send info.** Earn one qualifying answer first, then the info
    email.

Competitive hooks to weave in (never as the lead):
- **Extron SMP is EOL** — "What's your plan when support ends?"
- **Matrox exited the encoder market** — abandoned-platform angle.
- **vMix/OBS are software** — unreliable for mission-critical unattended
  recording; Pearl is dedicated hardware built for one job.

## When to Use vs Call Playbook

Use THIS (email/SMS) when:
- No phone number, or dials aren't connecting.
- The prospect prefers written contact, or replied by email.
- You're running the async legs of a Nooks sequence.
- You need to send info AFTER earning a qualifying answer.

Use epiphan-call-playbook (phone) when:
- You have a good number and a live-dial window.
- A prospect replied and is ready to talk — book or run the call.
- You need real-time discovery instead of a one-question-per-touch drip.

Hand-off: when email earns a reply, pass the context to epiphan-call-playbook
so the call opens where the thread left off — never restart discovery from zero.
