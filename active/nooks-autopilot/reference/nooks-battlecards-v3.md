**Owner:** Tim Kipper | **Date:** 2026-05-29 | **Source docs:** `Epiphan_Killer_Questions_Branded.pdf` + `Epiphan_Discovery_Master_All_Verticals.xlsx`

## What changed vs. v2

Every battlecard now follows the **3-step killer-question arc** so an SDR can run it on a 60-second dial without thinking:

1. **Step 1 — PAIN:** Open with the vertical-specific pain stem. "Biggest challenge that, if still broken a year from now, would hurt X."
2. **Step 2 — NEED:** "What's making this a priority right now?"
3. **Step 3 — FUTURE:** "If X just worked, what would that free your team up to do?"
4. **Bridge to demo:** "Can I try a few ideas on you?" → surface ONE Epiphan-unique capability that rigs buying criteria.

Each battlecard now has:
- The 3 stems pre-written so the rep doesn't ad-lib
- The single Epiphan capability to surface in step 4 (the "rig")
- Cost-of-inaction math (from the Discovery Master) the rep can drop if the prospect wants numbers
- Closing meeting-set ask

Memorization aid: **"Biggest challenge that hurts X" → "what's making it a priority now" → "if X just worked, what's that free up" → "can I try a few ideas on you" → drop the one Epiphan capability → ask for the demo.**

## 1. Live Event Producer

**Step 1 — Pain:** "What's the biggest risk in your live production workflow right now that, if it went wrong on a major show, would damage the client relationship or your reputation?"

**Step 2 — Need:** "What's driving the pressure to make every stream rock-solid right now?"

**Step 3 — Future:** "If you never had to worry about a dropped feed or a failed multi-camera switch mid-event, what bigger work could your team take on?"

**Bridge → "Can I try a few ideas on you?"**

**The ONE Epiphan capability to surface:** Hardware-encoded SRT contribution with local recording backup independent of the production switcher.
- "Imagine a dedicated hardware encoder at the source that records locally as a backup. Even if your switcher PC crashes, the recording is safe. SRT contribution adjusts to venue bandwidth automatically. To what degree would that change your risk profile on a major show?"

**Cost-of-inaction (if they want numbers):** One failed broadcast = $20K–$100K in sponsor penalties, distribution refunds, and league/client reputation. Three avoidable failures per year pays for the rig.

**Close:** "Sounds like hardware backup and SRT reliability are the priorities. Worth a 30-minute demo with one of our SEs to see it in your workflow?"

# How to use this in Nooks

1. **Paste each battlecard into the matching persona's Battlecard tab.** Nooks shows it on-screen during a connected call.
2. **Trim if needed:** Nooks battlecards are best at 8–12 lines visible without scrolling. The 3 step questions + the bridge + the one capability is the must-keep. Cost-of-inaction goes second. Close goes third.
3. **Format suggestion in Nooks:**
   - **Bold the 3 step questions** so the rep can find them on a live call
   - Italicize the Epiphan capability line so it stands out as the "rig"
   - Keep the cost-of-inaction as a single line, not bullets
4. **Pair with persona pain points** (from v2 doc) — pains drive what the rep listens for in Step 1, battlecard tells them what to say in Step 4.
